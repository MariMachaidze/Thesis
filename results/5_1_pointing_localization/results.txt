5.1 Pointing Localization Accuracy — Results
=============================================

This section reports the accuracy and temporal performance of the
pointing localization pipeline. The evaluation was carried out on a
3 x 3 target grid printed on the workspace sheet (850 mm wide by 600 mm
deep). The operator pointed at each of the nine targets from three
different hand heights above the surface — 10 cm, 30 cm, and 50 cm —
with five independent trials per (target, height) combination, yielding
135 trial-level estimates and approximately 18 000 individual per-frame
samples. The localization error for each trial is defined as the
Euclidean distance, in millimetres, between the temporally smoothed
estimate produced by the pipeline and the true target position on the
workspace plane.


Data Curation
-------------

Two systematic problems were identified in the raw dataset and corrected
before any statistics were computed.

Two trials at the top-left target (T1) at a hand height of 30 cm
(trial 4 and trial 5) were excluded as tracking failures. In both cases
the temporal smoothing filter locked onto a stray detection located
near the opposite corner of the workspace, producing errors above
500 mm together with very low per-trial sample counts; these
measurements are not representative of normal system behaviour.

Two further trials were reassigned to the neighbouring target. At
30 cm hand height, trial 1 for T6 was relabelled as T5, and trial 1
for T9 was relabelled as T8. The measured positions for these two
trials lay very close to the ground-truth positions of T5 and T8
respectively, indicating that the operator pointed at the wrong
target during the run. The corresponding errors were recomputed
against the corrected ground-truth values and the trials retained.

After these corrections the data set contains 133 trial-level
localization estimates.


Overall Localization Accuracy
-----------------------------

Across the curated data set (N = 133), the system produced a mean
localization error of 63.9 mm with a standard deviation of 30.1 mm and
a median of 60.0 mm. Errors ranged from a minimum of 3.0 mm to a
maximum of 177.3 mm. The proximity between mean and median, together
with the modest standard deviation, indicates that the error
distribution is approximately unimodal and only mildly skewed once the
gross tracking failures have been removed. Relative to the 850 x 600 mm
operational area, the mean error corresponds to roughly 7-8% of the
workspace's smaller dimension, which is adequate for the coarse
selection of regions and waypoints required by the downstream Sphero
navigation task.


Effect of Hand Height
---------------------

Localization accuracy was analysed separately for each of the three
hand heights. The mean error was 81.6 mm (std 19.3 mm, median 81.2 mm)
at 10 cm, 51.9 mm (std 35.3 mm, median 42.2 mm) at 30 cm, and 57.6 mm
(std 25.4 mm, median 56.1 mm) at 50 cm. Accuracy did not therefore
degrade monotonically with hand height; on the contrary, the lowest
hand position produced the largest mean error and the elevated
positions both improved on it noticeably. This behaviour can be
explained geometrically: when the hand is very close to the workspace,
the pointing vector — defined between two finger landmarks separated
by only a few centimetres — is short and any depth jitter on either
landmark causes a disproportionate angular change in the projected
ray. At greater hand heights the same physical jitter spans a longer
baseline and the resulting projection on the workspace plane is more
stable. The 30 cm condition emerged as the most favourable in terms
of central tendency, while 50 cm offered the tightest dispersion
around its mean.


Spatial Accuracy Across the Workspace
-------------------------------------

The mean error was then aggregated per target and arranged according
to the physical 3 x 3 layout. The largest mean errors were found
along the middle-left region of the workspace: target T4 (mid-left)
showed the highest mean error at 94.6 mm, followed by T2 (top-centre)
at 72.4 mm and T5 (centre) at 69.8 mm. The remaining targets all fell
in a relatively narrow band between approximately 48 and 70 mm, with
T8 (bottom-centre) the most accurate at 48.0 mm. There is therefore a
systematic, but not large, tendency for the left-hand column of the
workspace to be less accurately localized than the right-hand column.
This is consistent with two well-known characteristics of
single-camera vision-based pointing systems: peripheral targets are
imaged at oblique angles, which reduces the effective depth
resolution along the pointing ray, and any residual calibration
offset between the depth sensor and the workspace plane is amplified
at locations farther from the calibration centre. The axis-bias
analysis confirmed a consistent leftward offset of the estimates at
all three hand heights, which contributes to the slightly poorer
accuracy of the left-hand targets.


Sources of Larger Errors
------------------------

The residual heavy tail of the error distribution can be attributed
to three principal factors. First, natural unsteadiness of the human
hand during sustained pointing produces high-frequency jitter that
the One-Euro temporal filter mitigates but does not entirely remove.
Second, occasional partial occlusions or merging of the fingertip
with the wrist or background structure cause isolated misdetections
that are propagated through the filter when the trial is short.
Third, the oblique viewing geometry at the periphery of the
workspace reduces the resolution of the depth sensor along the
pointing ray, increasing the variance of the projected estimate at
peripheral targets such as T4. Notably, even the corrected maximum
error of 177.3 mm is well below the workspace's half-width, so the
worst-case estimate still selects the correct quadrant of the
workspace.


Real-Time Usability
-------------------

In addition to spatial accuracy, the temporal performance of the
pipeline was characterised at the per-sample level. Across all
retained trials (N ≈ 18 000 samples) the mean processing latency per
frame was 55.4 ms with a standard deviation of 16.7 ms; the bulk of
the distribution lay between 45 and 65 ms, corresponding to an
effective update rate of approximately 15-20 Hz. The pipeline ran
continuously throughout the experiment with no detected frame drops
or processing stalls, and the latency distribution remained stable
across all three hand heights. A response time in this range is well
within the limits commonly accepted for real-time human-robot
interaction and is sufficient to drive the downstream Sphero
navigation system without introducing perceivable lag for the user.


Summary
-------

After excluding two filter-lock failures and re-labelling two trials
in which the operator pointed at the wrong target, the pointing
localization system achieved a mean error of 63.9 mm and a median of
60.0 mm across 133 trials, with errors bounded by 3-177 mm. The 30 cm
hand height produced the best central tendency, the 50 cm height the
tightest variance, and the 10 cm height the worst overall accuracy —
indicating that performance does not decrease with hand height but in
fact benefits from a longer pointing baseline. Spatial accuracy was
broadly uniform across the workspace, with a modest but systematic
penalty at left-hand and edge targets attributable to peripheral
viewing geometry and a small residual leftward axis bias. The
measured per-frame latency of approximately 55 ms confirms that the
localization stage operates comfortably in real time, and the
combination of stable latency, modest mean error, and absence of
filter divergence (once the two tracking failures are removed)
indicates that the system delivers repeatable and sufficiently
accurate pointing performance for the interaction scenario targeted
in this thesis.
