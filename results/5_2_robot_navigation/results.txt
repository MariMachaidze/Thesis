================================================================================
Section 5.2 — Robot Navigation Accuracy
Gesture-Based Teleoperation of a Sphero Mobile Robot
================================================================================

Dataset
-------
The analysis is based on 275 arrived trials extracted from 16 recording
sessions stored in bags/thesis_final/. Each trial corresponds to one user
pointing gesture that was successfully committed by the gating logic
(t_start) together with the subsequent first continuous in-square run of
the Sphero robot. The per-trial metric is the minimum Euclidean distance,
in centimetres, between the robot centre and the user's pointed
coordinate during the first in-square run that begins after t_start;
pre-parked runs left over from a previous trial are skipped so the
metric reflects new navigation effort. The pointed coordinate is clipped
to the navigable workspace x in [5, 80] cm, y in [5, 55] cm.

Success is operationalised at a 5 cm radius, matching the upstream
summary file. This threshold is small enough to be perceptually
meaningful for tabletop interaction (it is comparable to the diameter
of the Sphero itself) and large enough to absorb the residual
single-frame jitter introduced by the vision pipeline.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Overall navigation accuracy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Across all 275 arrived trials, the mean minimum in-square distance was
3.10 cm (SD = 2.32 cm; median = 2.62 cm; min = 0.13 cm; max = 10.21 cm).
The median is below the mean, indicating a right-skewed distribution
in which the bulk of trials cluster close to the target and a smaller
tail of trials accumulates several centimetres of residual error.

Looking at the cumulative breakdown:

    Within 2 cm : 40.7 %   (112 / 275)
    Within 5 cm : 77.8 %   (214 / 275)
    Within 8 cm : 97.1 %   (267 / 275)

The 5 cm threshold is reached in roughly four out of five trials,
which is consistent with a system whose dominant error source is
the pointing estimate rather than the navigation controller: the
upstream pointing-localisation evaluation (Section 5.1) reports
single-shot pointing errors of the same order, and the navigation
stage only has to converge to that already-imperfect target.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Success rate
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Adopting nav_accuracy_cm <= 5 cm as the operational success criterion,
the success rate is 77.8 % (failure rate 22.2 %). Within the broader
8 cm tolerance the success rate rises to 97.1 %, and only 2.9 % of
trials remain beyond that envelope. Per-session means range from
1.77 cm to 3.99 cm, indicating that no single recording dominates the
aggregate and that the system is reproducible across users and days.
Per-square means are likewise tightly grouped: every grid cell that
saw at least four arrivals had a mean error between 1.86 cm and
4.94 cm, with no obvious centre-vs-corner asymmetry that would point
to a systematic mechanical bias.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Latency
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Latency, measured as the time from t_start to the detection at which
the minimum distance was attained, averaged 6.93 s (SD = 4.97 s;
median = 5.57 s). The minimum was 0.13 s — trials in which the robot
was already very close to the new target when the gesture was
committed — and the maximum was 32.6 s, reflecting a handful of
long-traversal cases where the Sphero had to cross most of the
workspace or briefly mis-tracked before converging. The fact that the
median (5.57 s) is well below the mean (6.93 s) again confirms a
right-skewed distribution: the majority of approaches complete in
under six seconds, but a non-trivial tail of long approaches inflates
the mean. For an interactive table-top teleoperation system this
latency profile is acceptable: it is dominated by physical traversal
time rather than by perception or control overhead.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Trajectory observations
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The per-session trajectory plot (robot_trajectory_plot.png) overlays
the ordered sequence of pointed coordinates for every session. Two
qualitative patterns are visible. First, the dispersion of reached
points around each target square centre is small (typically under
~3 cm), which is the spatial signature of the histogram results.
Second, paths sweep through neighbouring squares rather than
teleporting across the workspace, confirming that the Sphero
genuinely tracks the pointed coordinate and is not simply landing
on a discrete grid.

The error-over-time plot (trajectory_error_over_time.png) plots
per-trial nav_accuracy_cm against time since the first trial in each
session. There is no monotonic drift across a session — the curves
oscillate around the session mean — which suggests that calibration
remained stable for the duration of recording and that there is no
appreciable thermal or mechanical drift in the camera, the projector,
or the Sphero's IMU.

The target-vs-reached scatter (target_vs_robot_scatter.png) plots the
target coordinate against the coordinate actually reached, separately
for the x- and y-axes, with the diagonal indicating the ideal
target = robot relation. The points lie close to the diagonal across
the full workspace, with successes (green) tightly clustered and
the failure subset (red) scattered symmetrically on both sides of
the diagonal. The symmetric scatter is important: it shows that the
residual error does not exhibit a consistent bias (the robot is
not systematically overshooting or undershooting), and is therefore
better described as zero-mean noise than as a controller offset.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Navigation failures
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sixty-one trials (22.2 %) exceeded the 5 cm criterion, and only
eight trials (2.9 %) exceeded 8 cm. Inspection of these cases against
the per-square breakdown and the latency distribution suggests three
contributing factors, none of which dominates on its own.

(a) Overshoot at high commanded velocities. The Sphero's response
    behaviour is essentially a PD controller acting on the projected
    target. When the pointed coordinate lies near the far edge of the
    workspace, the robot accelerates strongly and a fraction of trials
    overshoot the target by 1–3 cm before settling. Because the
    metric records the minimum distance attained while still in the
    target square, very brief overshoots that exit the square are not
    fully captured; they instead surface as elevated nav_accuracy_cm
    for short in-square runs.

(b) Late stabilisation. Some trials show a long latency (>15 s)
    together with a modest residual error. These correspond to cases
    in which the next pointing gesture arrived before the previous
    approach had fully settled, so the controller was already chasing
    a moving target when t_start was committed. This is a property
    of the experimental protocol (free-flowing pointing) rather than
    of the navigation stage itself.

(c) Pointing-coordinate noise. Because the navigation reference is
    the averaged pointed coordinate, the navigation error inherits
    the variance of the pointing pipeline. Failure trials are
    distributed across the workspace rather than being concentrated
    in a particular grid cell, which is consistent with pointing
    noise being the dominant residual source.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. Summary
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The gesture-driven teleoperation pipeline delivers a navigation
accuracy of 3.10 +/- 2.32 cm with a 77.8 % within-5-cm success rate
and a median latency of 5.6 s over 275 trials and 16 sessions.
Trajectory plots confirm stable tracking with no within-session
drift, and target-vs-reached scatter shows symmetric, zero-mean
residuals across the workspace. The remaining failure cases are
attributable to a combination of overshoot at high commanded
velocities, late stabilisation under rapid successive gestures, and
inherited pointing-coordinate noise; none of these point to a
systematic deficiency of the navigation controller. The system is
therefore well-suited to coarse, room-scale tele-pointing tasks,
and any future tightening of the accuracy envelope is most likely
to come from improvements upstream of navigation rather than from
the motion controller itself.
